This scenario evaluates the prototype in a collision risk analysis task where qualitative confidence levels are not provided in advance and must instead be assigned by the user during the interpretation process. As in the previous case, the situation involves two satellites under apparent conjunction concern. The first satellite is represented by a well constrained orbit with recent observation support and a small uncertainty ellipsoid. The second satellite is represented by two alternative trajectories from different sources. The purpose of the scenario is to assess whether the operator can examine the behaviour of each estimate, judge its credibility, and use the interface to assign an explicit confidence classification that supports the final conclusion.

In the proposed data, one of the trajectories for the second satellite indicates a collision risk with the first satellite, but it does so under conditions that should raise doubt. This first estimate is associated with a very old last observation time, so that a long interval separates the most recent direct observation from the predicted time of closest approach. Despite this large temporal gap, its uncertainty ellipsoid remains unrealistically small and does not grow in a meaningful way as time advances. The second estimate, by contrast, is associated with a more recent observation and shows a pattern of uncertainty growth that is more consistent with predictive propagation over time. This second trajectory does not indicate a relevant collision risk. The scenario is therefore designed so that the more alarming estimate is also the one whose internal behaviour appears less credible.

The expected reasoning process is that the user will inspect the last observation information, compare the uncertainty evolution of the two trajectories, and recognize that the first estimate should not be trusted to the same degree as the second. In this case, the important interpretive step is not simply choosing between two displayed paths, but understanding why one source deserves a lower qualitative confidence assessment. The prototype supports this by making the observation status and uncertainty pattern visually available in the same environment and by allowing the user to assign a confidence classification directly to the trajectory under inspection. This transforms the scenario from a passive reading of source quality into an active judgement task supported by the interface.

The intended conclusion is that the collision alert should be dismissed because it is driven mainly by the less credible trajectory. The user is expected to classify that trajectory with low confidence on the basis of its unrealistic uncertainty behaviour and its distant last observation, and then to prioritize the alternative estimate that behaves more plausibly and does not indicate dangerous proximity. This scenario therefore complements the previous one by evaluating not only whether the software can display qualitative source trust, but also whether it can support the operator in constructing that trust judgement directly from the available visual evidence. In this way, the prototype is assessed as a tool not only for reading source credibility, but also for helping form it.
