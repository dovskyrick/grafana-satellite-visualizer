This scenario evaluates the prototype in a diagnostic task involving degraded communication between a satellite and a ground station. The operator is presented with telemetry time series that indicate a communication anomaly during a contact interval that would normally be expected to support stable data exchange. The purpose of the scenario is to assess whether the user can move from the detection of the anomaly in the temporal plots to a spatial interpretation of its likely cause. Rather than treating the event as an abstract drop in link quality, the task requires the operator to relate the moment of degradation to the geometric state of the spacecraft and to the directional behavior of the relevant antenna.

In the proposed data, the anomaly is visible through a deterioration in communication related telemetry such as received signal strength, packet success rate, or a general link quality indicator. The user is expected to identify the relevant interval in the time series and select the moment at which the fault becomes most evident. Once that temporal position is selected, the synchronized spatial view shows the spacecraft attitude, the antenna pointing direction, and the corresponding projected footprint on the Earth. The scene is configured so that, at the time of degraded communication, the antenna footprint no longer aligns with the ground station that should be receiving the signal. This creates a clear spatial inconsistency that can be inspected directly in the interface.

The reasoning task in this scenario is therefore one of cross domain correlation. The user must first detect the anomaly in the telemetry domain and then use the synchronized geometric view to evaluate whether the spacecraft remained properly oriented for communication. The prototype supports this by linking the selected time in the plot to the spacecraft state shown in the spatial panel, allowing the operator to inspect attitude and Earth directed pointing without leaving the dashboard context. The expected interpretation is that the communication problem is not best explained as an isolated ground segment failure, but as a consequence of spacecraft orientation or antenna mispointing during the relevant interval.

The intended conclusion is that the degraded link is associated with incorrect directional alignment between the spacecraft antenna and the ground station. By observing that the footprint of the relevant antenna is displaced from the ground station at the same moment in which communication quality falls, the user can form a plausible diagnosis grounded in both telemetry and geometry. This scenario therefore evaluates one of the central claims of the prototype: that time series anomalies become easier to interpret when they can be inspected together with attitude and pointing context in the same operational environment. It also illustrates the value of the system for tasks in which temporal evidence alone is insufficient to explain the operational cause of an event.
